## Introduction

In Slovakia, getting an ADHD diagnosis as an adult remains a major challenge. Although the topic is increasingly discussed on social media, the reality in Slovak healthcare practices can look very different. If you are over thirty and begin to suspect that the lifelong chaos, procrastination and difficulty maintaining attention you have experienced are not simply laziness but may be related to a neurodevelopmental disorder, you may be facing a very long journey.

## Why is it so difficult?

There are too few specialists... and that is only the tip of the iceberg. If you try to make an appointment with a psychiatrist who specializes in adult ADHD, you may quickly encounter the harsh reality.

Here are three main reasons why the process can be so complicated in Slovakia:

### 1. Lack of specialists for adults

Historically, ADHD (also known in the past as LMD – minimal brain dysfunction) was viewed primarily as a condition affecting hyperactive boys that people would "grow out of" with age. Modern research has long shown that ADHD can persist into adulthood.

The problem is that **child psychiatrists**, who commonly diagnose ADHD in children, generally stop providing care within the paediatric system once a patient reaches adulthood. **Adult psychiatrists** in Slovakia may have varying levels of experience with ADHD assessment in adults, as it was not always a major part of their earlier training. As a result, the relatively small number of clinicians who focus on adult ADHD may have **waiting times of 6 to 12 months**, or may have stopped accepting new patients altogether.

### 2. Diagnostic confusion (Misdiagnosis)

Because ADHD symptoms in adults can present differently from those typically recognized in children — including inner restlessness, emotional dysregulation, burnout and anxiety — they may sometimes be attributed to other conditions.

Adults with undiagnosed ADHD may receive diagnoses such as:
* Depression and anxiety disorders
* Bipolar affective disorder (BAP)
* Borderline personality disorder (BPD)

When treatment focuses only on these conditions while ADHD remains unrecognized, the underlying difficulties may not be fully addressed, which can lead to further frustration for the patient.

> "I had been treated for an anxiety-depressive disorder since university. Antidepressants never helped with the constant chaos in my head. It was only at 32 that a private psychologist confirmed ADHD. Suddenly, everything made sense." – *Peter (34), Bratislava*

### 3. Bureaucratic and financial barriers

If you do not want to wait a year within the public healthcare system, the **private sector** may be an alternative. A full assessment, which can include a clinical psychological examination, standardized tests and psychiatric evaluation, may cost **€200 to €500**.

And even if you receive a diagnosis, that does not automatically solve everything. Access to effective stimulant medications, such as methylphenidate, is subject to legal and administrative restrictions. Some medications commonly used abroad may not be routinely reimbursed by Slovak health insurance for adults, and prescribing certain ADHD medications can involve additional administrative requirements.

## Comparison: Public vs. Private Sector

* **Waiting time:**
    * *Public clinic:* 6 to 14 months (often not accepting new patients)
    * *Private clinic:* 1 to 3 months
* **Cost of assessment:**
    * *Public clinic:* Free of charge (covered by health insurance)
    * *Private clinic:* €200 – €500
* **Access to newer diagnostic methods:**
    * *Public clinic:* May rely on older questionnaires
    * *Private clinic:* Modern diagnostic batteries (e.g. DIVA-5)

## What should you do if you suspect ADHD?

If you feel that your symptoms may be consistent with ADHD, do not let the first rejection discourage you. Approach the process systematically:

1. **Complete a self-screening questionnaire:** Official screening questionnaires, such as the ASRS (Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale), are freely available online. They can help you organize your thoughts and identify patterns worth discussing with a professional.
2. **Write down symptoms from childhood:** For an ADHD diagnosis, it is important to establish that symptoms were already present during childhood. Old school reports, report cards or assessments may provide useful information.
3. **Look for recommendations within communities:** On social media (Facebook groups, Reddit), there are Slovak ADHD communities where people share their experiences and recommend clinicians they have worked with.
4. **Contact a clinical psychologist:** The assessment does not necessarily have to begin with a psychiatrist. A qualified clinical psychologist can perform a psychological assessment, after which you can take the results to a psychiatrist for further evaluation and treatment planning.
5. **Prepare for the assessment:** Write down specific examples from everyday life (forgetting things, losing items, difficulty managing time and organizing tasks) so that you do not forget anything important when you are under stress.

## Conclusion

Getting an ADHD diagnosis as an adult in Slovakia can require a great deal of patience and, unfortunately, financial resources — which is particularly frustrating when difficulties with organization and managing tasks are themselves common features of ADHD.

A diagnosis, however, is not simply a "label". For many people, it can be a **moment of relief**, when they finally understand that they are not incapable or lazy, but that their brain works differently. Change within Slovak healthcare is gradual, but as awareness grows, access to understanding and appropriate support can improve.
