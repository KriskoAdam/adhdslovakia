# ADHD Medications in Slovakia: How Do Concerta, Strattera, Intuniv and the Newer Livizux Work?

**ADHD treatment in Slovakia has advanced significantly in recent years. A newer option, Livizux, has joined the traditional treatments, and patients now have more options than ever before. How do these medications work, how do they differ, and who are they intended for?**

The idea that ADHD medications are simply "miracle pills for concentration" or even a form of legal doping is one of the most widespread myths. Modern neuroscience, however, shows something quite different.

ADHD is not a lack of discipline or willpower. It is a neurodevelopmental disorder in which the brain processes neurotransmitters differently. This is why a person with ADHD may struggle to maintain attention, finish tasks or regulate impulsive behaviour despite genuinely trying.

ADHD medications do not create new abilities or change a person's personality. Their purpose is to help the brain use the mechanisms it already has more effectively.

> ADHD treatment is not "performance doping". It is a tool that helps compensate for neurochemical differences in the brain.

---

## What Happens in the Brain with ADHD?

The **prefrontal cortex** plays a key role — it is the part of the brain responsible for planning, organization, working memory, self-control and attention.

For it to function properly, it needs optimal levels of two neurotransmitters:

### Dopamine: The Filter for Important Information

Dopamine helps the brain determine which stimuli are important and which can be ignored. It acts as a filter for the surrounding noise.

### Noradrenaline: The Attention Amplifier

Noradrenaline helps maintain mental energy, concentration and the ability to complete a task without becoming distracted.

In ADHD, these neurotransmitters may be regulated differently, affecting the brain's ability to control attention, impulses and motivation.

Modern pharmacological treatment targets precisely these mechanisms.

## What ADHD Medications Are Available in Slovakia?

Medications used to treat ADHD can be divided into two main groups:

### Stimulants
- Methylphenidate (Concerta)
- Lisdexamfetamine (Livizux)

### Non-stimulants
- Atomoxetine (Strattera and generics)
- Guanfacine (Intuniv)

---

# Stimulants

Stimulants are considered a first-line treatment for ADHD worldwide and have consistently shown high effectiveness in reducing ADHD symptoms.

## Methylphenidate (Concerta)

### How Does It Work?

Methylphenidate belongs to a group of medications that inhibit the reuptake of dopamine and noradrenaline.

Imagine a synapse as a room where dopamine helps organize information. In ADHD, the brain may clear these neurotransmitters from the synapse too quickly. Methylphenidate blocks the transporters responsible for their reuptake.

The result is increased availability of dopamine and noradrenaline in brain regions involved in attention and self-control.

### What Makes Concerta Different?

Concerta uses **OROS** technology, which allows the active ingredient to be released gradually over approximately 10 to 12 hours.

The tablet contains a microscopic laser-drilled opening. Osmotic pressure gradually releases the medication throughout the day.

### Most Common Side Effects

- Decreased appetite
- Insomnia
- Headaches
- Rebound effects as the medication wears off

---

## Lisdexamfetamine (Livizux)

Livizux is one of the newer developments in ADHD treatment in Slovakia. It is intended particularly for patients for whom methylphenidate has not provided sufficient benefit or has caused unwanted side effects.

### How Does It Work?

It is what is known as a **prodrug**.

The patient takes an inactive molecule that is converted into its active form through metabolic processes in the body. This results in a relatively smooth and predictable release of the active substance over many hours.

This mechanism can reduce fluctuations in the effects of the medication during the day.

### Reimbursement in Slovakia

Livizux is covered by public health insurance for children and adolescents aged 6 to 18 whose treatment with methylphenidate has been clinically insufficient.

Adult patients have legal access to the medication, but in many cases they currently have to pay for it themselves.

### Most Common Side Effects

- Dry mouth
- Headaches
- Decreased appetite
- Weight loss
- Increased heart rate

# Non-Stimulants

Non-stimulants are used particularly when stimulants are not suitable or are not well tolerated.

This may include patients with significant anxiety, tics or certain cardiovascular limitations.

## Atomoxetine (Strattera)

Atomoxetine is an **NRI (selective norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor)**.

Unlike stimulants, it does not have an immediate onset of action. Its full effect usually develops over 2 to 4 weeks of regular treatment.

One advantage is a stable effect throughout the day without pronounced fluctuations.

### Most Common Side Effects

- Nausea
- Stomach problems
- Fatigue
- Drowsiness
- Sexual side effects in some adult patients

---

## Guanfacine (Intuniv)

Guanfacine works through a completely different mechanism from the other ADHD medications.

It is an **alpha-2A adrenergic receptor agonist** that directly affects neural connections in the prefrontal cortex.

It can be particularly useful for:

- hyperactivity,
- impulsivity,
- emotional dysregulation,
- difficulties with emotional regulation.

### Most Common Side Effects

- Significant drowsiness
- Fatigue
- Decreased blood pressure
- Slower heart rate

## What Should You Watch Out For?

Every person with ADHD responds to treatment differently. The process of finding the optimal medication and dose is called **titration** and can take several weeks to months.

When taking stimulants, it is advisable to limit or completely avoid energy drinks and excessive amounts of caffeine.

With atomoxetine, taking the medication with food may help reduce the risk of stomach problems.

Intuniv should never be stopped suddenly without consulting a doctor, as this can lead to a significant increase in blood pressure.

---

## Conclusion

ADHD medications do not permanently eliminate the underlying condition. They work more like prescription glasses for the brain.

When properly adjusted, they can significantly improve attention, organization, emotional regulation and everyday functioning. Combined with psychotherapy, psychoeducation and healthy habits, medication is one of the most effective tools available for the treatment of ADHD.

> Medication is not a failure. For many people with ADHD, it can be a way to regain greater control over their lives.
