Imagine being an adult who has struggled for years with difficulties
concentrating, managing time, impulsivity, forgetfulness or completing
everyday tasks. Perhaps you have spent your whole life being told that
you are lazy, unreliable, undisciplined or that you simply need to try
harder.

Eventually, you begin to wonder whether **ADHD** could explain some of
these difficulties. You seek professional help and start looking for a
psychiatrist or clinical psychologist who assesses ADHD in adults.

And then you encounter reality.

Some private clinics charge several hundred euros for an assessment.
Published price lists can include fees approaching **€600**.

For someone on an average income, a single parent, a student or anyone
already facing financial difficulties, this can become an almost
insurmountable obstacle.

This is where the problem that **ADHD Slovakia** wants to address
begins.

**A professional assessment of health-related difficulties should not be
a privilege reserved for people with high incomes.**

### ADHD does not disappear when someone becomes an adult

**ADHD (attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder)** is a
neurodevelopmental disorder. It is not a lack of discipline, poor
upbringing or a fashionable label for everyday absent-mindedness.

In adults, ADHD can present in different ways. Some people struggle with
attention, organisation, planning and completing tasks. Others
experience more pronounced difficulties with impulsivity, inner
restlessness or emotional regulation.

These difficulties can affect employment, education, relationships,
financial management and the ability to manage everyday life.

Many people only begin to suspect they may have ADHD in adulthood. Some
recognise their difficulties after becoming parents; others do so after
problems at work, burnout or learning more about neurodevelopmental
conditions.

Adults deserve the same level of professional attention as children.

**The fact that ADHD was not recognised in childhood should not mean
that a person is left without the opportunity for a professional
assessment as an adult.**

### Having health insurance does not automatically mean that care is accessible

Slovakia has a system of public health insurance. Under certain
conditions, patients can access psychiatric and clinical psychology care
covered by public health insurance.

In practice, however, an important question arises: **What realistic
options are available to an adult who needs an ADHD assessment?**

There is a difference between a healthcare service being eligible for
reimbursement and a patient actually being able to find a provider who
offers it under a contract with their health insurer.

Patients often need to find out:

-   Which psychiatrists and clinical psychologists assess ADHD in
    adults?
-   Which clinics have contracts with their health insurer?
-   Which parts of the assessment are covered by public health
    insurance?
-   How long are the waiting times?
-   How much does a comprehensive assessment cost?
-   What should they do if no suitable provider is available in their
    region?
-   What is the difference between a psychological examination, an
    assessment report and a medical diagnosis?

Finding this information should not require weeks of searching online,
making phone calls and contacting dozens of clinics.

**Patients need a clear, understandable and accessible route to
professional help.**

### Financial barriers can deepen inequality in access to care

Imagine two people with similar difficulties.

The first has sufficient financial resources. They can pay for a private
assessment and, if necessary, attend several appointments at different
clinics.

The second lives from one payday to the next. They have a family, a
mortgage, debts or a low income. A fee of **€400 to €600** is simply
beyond their means.

Both may need a professional assessment, but their financial
circumstances are very different.

A person's financial situation should not determine whether they have a
realistic opportunity to access an assessment.

ADHD itself can also make financial management, planning and maintaining
employment more difficult. This creates a paradox for some people: those
who may need professional support can also find it difficult to afford
that support.

**Access to healthcare should not depend on the size of a patient's bank
balance.**

### We are not attacking private healthcare professionals

It is important to emphasise that our aim is not to undermine the work
of private psychiatrists, clinical psychologists or diagnostic clinics.

A comprehensive assessment requires professional training, experience,
time and, often, the use of psychometric tools. Private clinics have
operating costs and are entitled to fair compensation for their work.

The problem is not the existence of private assessments, nor the fact
that professionals charge for their services.

**The problem is the lack of an accessible alternative for patients who
cannot afford private care.**

We cannot expect private clinics to solve every shortcoming in the
public healthcare system.

We need a system in which patients have a realistic opportunity to
access a high-quality assessment without having to pay hundreds of euros
out of their own pockets.

### ADHD Slovakia: We do not just want to highlight the problem --- we want to help people

We understand that finding professional help can itself be difficult for
someone who suspects they may have ADHD.

They may not know where to start, whom to contact, which professionals
work with adults, whether they need a referral or how much an assessment
will cost.

That is why ADHD Slovakia focuses not only on awareness and information
about ADHD. Our aim is also to provide **practical tools and information
that help people navigate the system and find professional support.**

One concrete step is our **interactive map of clinics and professionals
in Slovakia**.

### We created a map to help patients find out where to turn

We want people to be able to start their search without having to work
everything out from scratch.

That is why we created a **map of clinics, psychiatrists, clinical
psychologists and support services** on the ADHD Slovakia website. These
may be relevant to people looking for help related to ADHD.

The map is intended to make it easier to explore available options and
find services by location.

Where available, we aim to provide useful information about each
service, including:

-   **Address and location** of the provider.
-   **Contact details** and appointment information.
-   **Pricing information** and whether the service is offered on a
    self-pay basis.
-   **Health insurance information**, where available.
-   **The provider's area of focus** and the type of services offered.

It is important to note that not every provider listed automatically
carries out ADHD assessments for adults. Some offer supportive or other
professional services. Patients should therefore confirm the scope of
services, age limits, fees and reimbursement arrangements directly with
the provider.

The map does not replace a professional assessment or an individual
recommendation from a healthcare professional. Its purpose is to **make
the first step towards finding help easier**.

**You can find the map on our website:**

[ADHD Slovakia -- Map of clinics and
professionals](https://www.adhdslovakia.eu/sk#diagnosis-map)

We believe practical tools like this can save people time, make it
easier to find professionals and help them obtain the information they
need.

We also want to expand and update the map over time. If you know of a
clinic or professional who is missing from it, please let us know.

**Our goal is to make sure that a person who suspects they may have ADHD
does not have to spend weeks figuring out where they can even begin.**

### What needs to change?

ADHD Slovakia believes that access to ADHD assessment for adults
deserves a systematic response.

We do not want to stop at describing the problem. We want to open up a
professional and public discussion and work towards practical solutions.

We therefore believe the following changes are necessary:

**1. Accessible assessments covered by public health insurance**

Patients should have a realistic opportunity to undergo the necessary
professional assessments through healthcare providers contracted by
health insurers. It must be made clear which parts of the assessment
process are covered and under what conditions.

**2. A publicly accessible directory of professionals and clinics**

Patients should be able to easily find information about psychiatrists
and clinical psychologists who assess ADHD in adults. The directory
should also state which health insurers each provider has a contract
with.

**3. Shorter waiting times**

Access to healthcare is not only about affordability. If someone has to
wait an unreasonably long time for a professional assessment, the
problem remains unresolved.

**4. Support for professional training**

Training for healthcare professionals in adult ADHD should be supported.
High-quality assessment requires an understanding of how ADHD presents
at different stages of life and the ability to distinguish it from other
conditions that may cause similar symptoms.

**5. Transparency from health insurers**

Patients need to know what assessment options are available under their
health insurance. Health insurers should provide clear information and
help patients find accessible contracted providers.

### What do health insurers and the Ministry of Health have to say?

We believe this issue needs to be raised directly with the institutions
that can influence access to healthcare in Slovakia.

We intend to contact all three Slovak health insurance companies and the
Ministry of Health of the Slovak Republic.

In particular, we want to ask:

-   How many contracted healthcare providers in Slovakia assess ADHD in
    adults?
-   Which assessment services are covered by public health insurance?
-   What options are available to a patient who cannot find an
    accessible provider in their region?
-   Is there data on the number of adults who have undergone an ADHD
    assessment?
-   Are health insurers or the Ministry planning specific measures to
    improve access to this care?

We do not want to settle for general statements. We need specific
information that will help identify shortcomings and develop solutions.

### We want to hear from you, too

ADHD Slovakia does not want to speak about patients without involving
patients themselves.

That is why we want to involve people who have personal experience of
trying to obtain an ADHD assessment as an adult.

We would like to know:

-   Was it difficult for you to find a professional who assesses ADHD in
    adults?
-   How many clinics or professionals did you have to contact?
-   Were you offered an assessment covered by health insurance, or did
    you have to pay privately?
-   If you paid privately, how much did the assessment cost?
-   How long did you wait for an appointment?
-   If you did not complete the assessment, was the cost one of the
    reasons?

Your experiences can help us build a realistic picture of the situation
in Slovakia.

We therefore plan to prepare an **anonymous nationwide survey on access
to ADHD assessment for adults**. We intend to compile the results into a
report and submit it to the relevant institutions.

Personal stories can be an important part of this initiative, but we
also want to support them with concrete data and professional
perspectives.

### This is the beginning, not the end

We do not want access to ADHD assessment to be discussed only
occasionally, whenever another story emerges about someone who could not
afford it.

We want this issue to become part of the wider professional discussion
about access to healthcare in Slovakia.

ADHD Slovakia is therefore launching an initiative focused on
**improving access to ADHD assessment for adults**.

We will communicate with health insurers, professional organisations,
healthcare professionals and the Ministry of Health of the Slovak
Republic. We will collect patients' experiences, evaluate available data
and advocate for concrete systemic solutions.

We are not asking for lower professional standards. We are not asking
professionals to work for free. We are asking for **a functioning system
in which patients have a realistic opportunity to access high-quality
professional care.**

Because an ADHD assessment should not depend on how much money you have
in your bank account.

**ADHD is not a luxury. Diagnosis must not be a privilege.**
